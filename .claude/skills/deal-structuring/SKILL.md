# Deal Structuring

Structure is how a price gap becomes a deal. Every mechanism transfers a specific risk to a specific party — name the risk before proposing the mechanism.

## Techniques

1. **Legal form selection** — Share purchase, asset purchase, statutory merger, scheme of arrangement or tender offer. Each differs in liability transfer, consent requirements, tax treatment, shareholder approval threshold and timetable. Choose from the binding constraint, not from precedent.
   *Fails when:* an asset deal is proposed without checking which contracts require counterparty consent to assign.

2. **Consideration design** — Cash, stock, mixed, deferred, earn-out, rollover, vendor loan note, preferred, CVR. Cash transfers certainty to the seller; stock shares post-close outcomes; deferred consideration finances the buyer from the seller's balance sheet.

3. **Valuation-gap bridging** — When the gap is about future performance, use an earn-out. When it is about a specific contingency, use an indemnity or escrow. When it is about the buyer's own value, use stock. Diagnose the gap's cause before selecting the instrument.

4. **Earn-out construction** — Define the metric (EBITDA invites manipulation; revenue invites margin destruction; milestones invite disputes), the measurement period, the ceiling and floor, the accounting conventions frozen at signing, the buyer's operating covenants during the period, and the dispute-resolution mechanism. Most earn-out litigation comes from an undefined accounting convention.

5. **Locked box versus completion accounts** — Locked box fixes equity value at a past balance-sheet date with a value-leakage regime and an interest ticker; completion accounts adjust at closing. Locked box gives certainty and speed; completion accounts give accuracy. State who bears the trading risk between signing and closing under each.

6. **Working capital adjustment** — Define the target as a normalised level derived from at least twelve months of data with the seasonality shown, the accounting policies frozen, the calculation mechanics, the dispute process and the true-up timetable. An unexamined "normal level" is where value leaks after the handshake.

7. **Debt-like items schedule** — Negotiate the list explicitly: debt, finance leases, pension deficit, deferred consideration on prior acquisitions, factored receivables, accrued bonuses, tax liabilities, capex creditors, restructuring provisions. The list is worth more than a point of multiple.

8. **Risk allocation architecture** — Warranties, indemnities, specific indemnities, escrow, holdback, W&I insurance and price reduction. Map each identified diligence risk to one mechanism and state the residual exposure after it.

9. **Tax structuring** — Step-up availability, deductibility of goodwill, withholding on cross-border flows, treaty access, exit taxes, stamp duty, VAT treatment, and the post-close operating structure. Coordinate with tax advisers early; the structure is expensive to change after heads of terms.

10. **Conditionality and certainty** — Each condition reduces closing certainty. Price the certainty: a bid with fewer conditions is worth more than its headline. Quantify the discount you would apply to a financing-conditional bid.

11. **Break fees and reverse break fees** — Size against sector precedent as a percentage of equity value, calibrated to the regulatory risk the buyer is asking the seller to bear. A reverse break fee is the price of an uncertain clearance.

12. **Management incentives** — Rollover percentage, incentive plan size, hurdle, vesting, leaver provisions and governance rights. Align the package with the value the plan requires management to deliver.

13. **Governance and minority protection** — Board composition, reserved matters, information rights, tag and drag, pre-emption, and exit mechanics. For any partial acquisition, these terms decide whether the stake is worth its arithmetic value.

14. **Regulatory pathway design** — Structure to minimise the review burden where legitimate: filing strategy, carve-outs, hold-separate arrangements, remedy pre-planning. Never structure to avoid a filing that is required.

15. **Structure comparison** — Present two or three complete structures side by side across value, certainty, timing, tax, risk retained and approval requirements. Recommend one and state the condition that would change the recommendation.

## Quality bar

- [ ] Every mechanism traced to the specific risk it allocates
- [ ] Tax implications confirmed with advisers, not assumed
- [ ] Consent and change-of-control requirements mapped before form is fixed
- [ ] Certainty priced, not just described
- [ ] Economic effect of each structure quantified in dollars

## Outputs

Structure comparison, consideration design, adjustment mechanics, risk-allocation map, conditionality assessment, tax structure summary, recommendation memo.
