# Investment Memo Writing

A memo exists to produce a decision. Structure it so a reader who stops after the first page still knows what you recommend and why.

## Techniques

1. **Recommendation first** — The first hundred words state what we recommend, at what price, on what structure, and the three reasons. Building to a conclusion is for essays, not for committees.

2. **Decision framing** — State precisely what is being approved: price, structure, funding, delegated authority, and conditions. Ambiguity here is how committees approve one thing and get another.

3. **Three-sentence thesis** — What this business is, why it wins, why now. If it takes more than three sentences, the thesis is not yet clear enough to underwrite.

4. **Pillar structure** — Break the thesis into three to five pillars, each with its claim, evidence, proving metric and the fact that would disprove it. The disproving fact is what separates an argument from an advertisement.

5. **Evidence discipline** — Every number carries a source and a period; every percentage carries a base; every forecast carries a driver. Sweep the document for figures that fail this test before anyone else does.

6. **Assertion audit** — Identify every claim stated without evidence and either supply the evidence or label it as judgement. Labelled judgement is credible; unlabelled judgement is what committees catch.

7. **Our case versus management's** — Table the two side by side with the reason for each haircut and the total value effect. This is the page that demonstrates independent underwriting.

8. **Hedge removal** — Strip hedges that avoid taking a position. "May potentially be expected to" means nothing. State the view and own it; genuine uncertainty is expressed as a range with probabilities, not as vague verbs.

9. **Risk-section discipline** — Eight specific risks, each quantified, mitigated and owned, with residual exposure stated. Then name the three that would actually kill the deal. Cut every generic risk.

10. **Pre-mortem inclusion** — Include the account of how this fails, written from three years out. Committees trust memos that have already argued against themselves.

11. **Devil's advocate test** — Write the 500-word case against the transaction using only facts already in the memo. If it is persuasive, the memo is incomplete — go back.

12. **Internal contradiction sweep** — Find where the thesis claims pricing power and the risk section concedes competitive intensity. Resolve, do not leave both standing.

13. **Number consistency check** — Every figure appearing more than once, in the body and the appendices, must agree. Committees notice, and one inconsistency undermines the rest.

14. **Q&A preparation** — Write the twenty questions the committee will ask with the answer and the supporting exhibit for each. Then check that the memo already answers the ones that should not need asking.

15. **Length discipline** — Cut to the length at which every remaining sentence affects the decision. Report what was removed so nobody wonders whether it was considered.

## Quality bar

- [ ] Recommendation in the first hundred words
- [ ] Every number sourced and dated
- [ ] Judgements labelled as judgements
- [ ] Risks quantified, owned and reduced to a top three
- [ ] Pre-mortem included
- [ ] All figures internally consistent including appendices
- [ ] Devil's advocate case written and answered

## Outputs

Executive summary, full memo, our-case-versus-management table, risk register, pre-mortem, Q&A pack, conditions and next steps.
