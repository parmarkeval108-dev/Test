# Pitchbook Construction

Build the argument before the pages. A deck is a storyline with exhibits attached, not exhibits with a storyline assumed.

## Techniques

1. **Objective definition** — Before outlining: what is this asking the client to do, who decides, what do they already believe, and what must change in their mind. Four answers, written down, before any page exists.

2. **Ten-sentence storyline** — Write the argument as ten sentences. Each becomes a section. An argument that does not hold in ten sentences will not hold in forty pages.

3. **Generic-page elimination** — Delete every page that would appear in a pitch to any other client in this sector. What survives is the actual pitch, and it is usually a third of the draft.
   *Fails when:* a deck leads with fifteen pages of market context the client knows better than the bank does.

4. **Page inventory approval** — Page number, title, single message, exhibit, data source, owner. Approved before anyone opens a template. This is where rework is prevented.

5. **One message per page** — If a page has three messages it is three pages, or two of them are cut. Density is not rigour.

6. **Action titles** — Every title is a conclusion, not a label. "Revenue growth has outpaced the sector for six consecutive quarters," not "Revenue growth." Cap at twelve words.

7. **Title-only flow test** — Read the titles in sequence with nothing else. If they do not make a complete argument, the deck does not flow. Fix the titles or the order before fixing the exhibits.

8. **Exhibit-to-message fit** — For each page, state the message, then check the exhibit proves it. A chart that is interesting but does not prove the title is a chart in the wrong deck.

9. **Chart-type selection** — Composition, comparison, distribution, correlation and change over time each have a correct form. State why the alternatives are worse before defending the chosen one.

10. **Data-ink reduction** — Remove gridlines, 3D effects, redundant legends and decorative colour. Every remaining mark should carry information.

11. **Annotation for the absent presenter** — Annotate the two or three things the audience would otherwise miss. Materials circulate without their presenter, and annotation is what survives the handover.

12. **Footnote discipline** — Every exhibit carries source, date, methodology and any adjustment. A page without a source line is not finished.

13. **Consistency sweep** — Fonts, sizes, colours, number formats, decimal places, units, date formats, legend positions, source-line format. Produce a defect list with page numbers rather than a general impression.

14. **Fact verification pass** — Verify every figure against its source and record page, figure, source and status. Anything unverified comes off the page before the meeting, not after the question.

15. **Objection mapping** — Write the five objections the client will raise, the page each relates to, and the response. If a response has no supporting page, build it into the appendix.

16. **Differentiation test** — Assume two other banks pitch with the same public data. Identify what in this deck they cannot replicate. If the answer is nothing, the pitch is not finished.

17. **Delivery brief** — The sixty-second opening, the three pages that must land, the pages to skip if time is short, and the close with a specific ask.

## Quality bar

- [ ] Storyline approved before pages built
- [ ] Every title is a conclusion under twelve words
- [ ] Every exhibit proves its title
- [ ] Every figure verified against its source
- [ ] Every page has a source line
- [ ] Titles alone make a complete argument
- [ ] Something in the deck the competition cannot replicate

## Outputs

Storyline, page inventory, built deck, exhibit source log, fact-verification table, objection map, delivery brief, leave-behind version.
