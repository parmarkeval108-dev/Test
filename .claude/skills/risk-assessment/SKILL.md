# Risk Assessment

Generic risks dilute real ones. Eight specific, quantified, owned risks beat thirty boilerplate bullets.

## Techniques

1. **Quantified risk register** — Each risk: description specific enough to be falsifiable, likelihood, impact in dollars, mitigation, residual exposure after mitigation, owner, and the indicator that would tell you it is materialising. A risk without a dollar figure has not been assessed.

2. **The three that kill it** — Identify the three risks that could actually destroy the investment thesis, with probabilities. Everything else is management, not risk. Boards remember three things.

3. **Pre-mortem** — Assume the deal has failed three years out and write the account of why, using only facts visible today. This surfaces risks the register misses because it reasons backwards from failure rather than forwards from a checklist.

4. **Thesis-dependency mapping** — For each pillar of the investment thesis, state the fact that would disprove it. Those facts are the risk register; anything not connected to the thesis is background noise.

5. **Value-at-risk by driver** — Quantify the value impact of each risk materialising and rank. This tells you where diligence money goes and which risks are worth paying to transfer.

6. **Mitigation-to-residual discipline** — Mitigation reduces risk, it does not remove it. State residual exposure after mitigation explicitly. A register where every risk is "mitigated" is not a register.

7. **Regulatory risk assessment** — Combined shares by product and geographic market, likely review path, remedy exposure, timeline, and the precedent in this sector. Convert to a probability of clearance and a probability-weighted timeline.

8. **Integration risk** — Assess complexity against the acquirer's demonstrated integration capability: systems overlap, cultural distance, geographic spread, concurrent integrations underway, and the acquirer's own record on its last three deals.

9. **Key-person risk** — Name the individuals, quantify what leaves with each, assess their likelihood of leaving given their post-close economics, and design the retention package against that.

10. **Financing risk** — Commitment certainty, conditions to funding, market flex, syndication risk, and the plan if markets close between signing and funding. Assess the acquirer's fallback, not just the commitment paper.

11. **Concentration risk** — Customer, supplier, product, geographic and key-contract concentration, each with the revenue at risk, the notice period and the replacement time.

12. **Contingent liability assessment** — Litigation, tax, environmental, regulatory, product and warranty exposures, each with quantum, probability, insurance coverage and the structural protection proposed.

13. **Cyclical exposure** — Position in the cycle, historical peak-to-trough in this business, and the effect on covenants and returns if the cycle turns during the hold period.

14. **Risk-adjusted recommendation** — Convert the register into a position: the risks that are acceptable, the risks that require a structural protection, the risks that need to be priced, and the risk that would cause you to walk.

## Quality bar

- [ ] Every risk quantified in dollars
- [ ] Every risk has a named owner and a leading indicator
- [ ] Residual exposure stated after mitigation
- [ ] Pre-mortem completed and its findings in the register
- [ ] Generic risks removed
- [ ] The walk-away condition stated explicitly

## Outputs

Risk register, top-three analysis, pre-mortem, value-at-risk ranking, mitigation and structural protection plan, walk-away conditions.
