# Synergy Quantification

A synergy without a driver, an owner and a date is a hope. Grade the evidence and let the grade decide what gets underwritten.

## Techniques

1. **Line-item inventory** — Every synergy gets: category, description, gross annual value, the driver it is computed from, full run-rate year, one-time cost to achieve, accountable owner, and an evidence grade. No line without a driver.

2. **Evidence grading** — A: contractually verifiable (public-company costs, duplicate leases, named contract terms). B: supported by data-room evidence. C: management assertion. D: adviser judgement. Underwrite A and B; disclose C and D separately.
   *Fails when:* a C-grade revenue synergy carries the same weight in the model as an A-grade cost elimination.

3. **Bottom-up build** — Headcount synergies from the combined org chart by role and level; procurement from category spend and the observed price differential; footprint from named sites and their receiving locations. Percentage-of-cost-base estimates are a benchmark, never a build.

4. **Public-company cost elimination** — Listing fees, audit, investor relations, board compensation, D&O, compliance. The most certain synergy class in any public take-out — size it precisely because it costs nothing in credibility.

5. **Revenue synergy discipline** — Addressable accounts, attach rate with the evidence supporting it, average deal size, gross margin, ramp period, and the sales capacity required. Then net customer attrition against it before presenting a figure.

6. **Dis-synergy quantification** — Customer overlap loss, channel conflict, attrition during integration, talent departure, change-of-control renegotiations and management distraction. A synergy case without dis-synergies is not a case, it is a pitch.

7. **Already-in-plan test** — Cross-check every cost synergy against the target's standalone cost programme. Savings already in their plan are in the price you are paying, not a benefit of the combination.

8. **Action-driven phasing** — Phase each line by the specific action required — notice periods, contract expiries, system cutover dates, regulatory clearance — rather than a smooth curve. Smooth curves are always optimistic in year one.

9. **Cost to achieve** — Severance, retention, facility exit, IT integration, systems, advisers, rebranding and dual-running, expressed as a multiple of run-rate synergies and benchmarked against comparable deals. Typical is 1.0–2.0x for cost synergies; below 0.5x needs an explanation.

10. **Three-number discipline** — What we underwrite, what we announce, and what we let the price reflect are three different numbers. State all three and the gap between them.

11. **Regulatory gating** — Identify synergies that antitrust review may prohibit and those that gun-jumping rules bar from being pursued before closing. Re-phase accordingly; a synergy that cannot start until clearance plus six months is not a year-one synergy.

12. **Value-split analysis** — Compute the percentage of risk-adjusted synergy NPV transferred to the seller through the premium. If the premium exceeds the synergy NPV, state what else must be true for value to be created.

13. **Tracker design** — Line items with owners, monthly targets, actuals, the validation rule that permits a saving to be booked, and an escalation trigger. Designed before close, or it does not happen.

14. **Precedent outcome study** — Study announced synergy targets in comparable deals against actual delivery. The typical shortfall and typical delay are the best prior available for this deal.

## Quality bar

- [ ] Every line has a driver, an owner and a full run-rate date
- [ ] Evidence graded, with underwritten total separated from announced total
- [ ] Dis-synergies quantified, not mentioned
- [ ] Costs to achieve benchmarked as a multiple of run-rate
- [ ] Overlap with the target's standalone plan tested and removed
- [ ] Total benchmarked against target cost base and comparable deals

## Outputs

Synergy inventory with grades, phasing schedule, cost-to-achieve schedule, NPV net of costs, dis-synergy schedule, announcement recommendation, tracker design.
