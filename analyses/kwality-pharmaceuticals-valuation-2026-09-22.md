The consequences, stated plainly:

**Nothing here should be relied on for a transaction or an investment decision until the FY26 annual

## 1. Conclusion

**Recommended value range: ₹1,900 – ₹2,900 per share** (equity ₹1,970 – ₹3,010 crore; EV ₹2,080 – ₹3,120 crore),
midpoint ₹2,400.

The share traded at **₹3,773.95 on 21 September 2026** (all-time high ₹3,797.5 the same day; 52-week range
₹1,561 – ₹3,798.90). The market price sits **~30% above the top of the range and ~57% above the midpoint**.

Three arguments support the range:

1. **The business is genuinely compounding.** Revenue CAGR of 26.1% FY23→FY26 (₹251.0 cr → ₹503.1 cr) with
   EBITDA margin up from ~22% to 24% and PAT up 69% in FY26 to ₹67.3 cr. This is not a one-year artefact,
   and the FY27 guidance of >₹700 cr with 27–28% EBITDA margin is supported by a Q1 FY27 that delivered
   ₹162 cr of revenue at a 25.3% EBITDA margin.
2. **But the earnings are not converting to cash.** FY26 operating cash flow was **₹17.0 cr against PAT of
   ₹67.3 cr** — 25% conversion — while standalone trade receivables **almost doubled to ₹281.2 cr**. On
   ₹503 cr of revenue that is ~204 days of sales outstanding. Working capital intensity (NWC/operating
   income) was 50.2% in FY25. Growth in this business consumes roughly half of every incremental rupee of
   revenue before a rupee of value is created.
3. **A cash-based valuation therefore lands far below an earnings-multiple valuation, and the gap is the
   whole story.** My base DCF, which charges growth for the working capital it actually absorbs and
   discounts at an Indian small-cap rate, values the equity at **₹955/share**. Peer trading multiples
   applied to FY27E earnings give **₹2,500 – ₹3,400/share**. The recommended range sits where the more
   optimistic (but internally consistent) DCF and the more conservative comps overlap — and above the base
   DCF because the market evidence in §8 cannot be dismissed, while the DCF's terminal assumptions punish
   a company whose capital cycle may genuinely be improving (the cash conversion cycle fell from 208 to
   170 days in FY26).

**At ₹3,774 the market is not paying for the FY27 guidance. It is paying for the FY30 target of ₹1,500 cr
at 30% EBITDA margins, delivered on time, with the working capital problem solved.** A reverse DCF (§7)
shows the price implies a discount rate of 8.7–11.2% on my operating cases — below what an Indian
micro-cap with 204 days of receivables in semi-regulated export markets should have to offer.

## 3. The business

Incorporated 1983, Amritsar. Manufacturer and exporter of finished formulations — liquid orals, powder
for oral suspension, tablets, capsules, sterile powder for injection, small-volume injectables, ointments,
ORS — across beta-lactam, non-beta-lactam, cytotoxic (oncology) and effervescent lines. Two campuses:
Nag Kalan, Majitha Road, Amritsar, and Jassur, Himachal Pradesh.

- **Product mix (Q1 FY27):** injectables 43% of revenue, tablets 40%, capsules 6%.
- **Markets:** exports ~50% of revenue (ICRA, May 2026), primarily semi-regulated — Africa, ASEAN, Gulf,
  and registration-led entries in Mexico and Colombia. Approvals cited include EU-GMP, PIC/S, INVIMA,
  COFEPRIS, DIGEMID, WHO (Amritsar), TMDA Tanzania, FDA Iraq, Iran GMP, Cambodia.
- **Pipeline / optionality:** hormone facility (capex ₹65 cr, ~50% incurred, targeted ~₹150 cr annual
  revenue) due to launch November 2026; oncology expansion by March 2027; CDSCO approval received for
  pilot batches of **Pembrolizumab**, with a Keytruda biosimilar targeted for commercial launch before
  end-2028. Total planned capex ₹185–190 cr excluding biosimilar trials.
- **Ownership:** promoters 54.86% (largest single holder Ramesh Arora, 26.01%); **promoter pledge 0.00%**,
  unchanged as at June 2026. Promoter control is unambiguous and unencumbered.
- **Credit:** ICRA upgraded the ratings in May 2026 and removed the issuer from the Issuer Not Cooperating
  category; rated bank facilities raised from ₹65 cr to ₹200 cr. *Judgement: the limit increase is as much
  a working-capital requirement as a vote of confidence — see §5.*

*Judgement:* the semi-regulated export mix is the source of both the margin expansion and the receivables
problem. Tender and distributor-led sales into Africa, ASEAN and Latin America carry long collection
cycles and concentrated counterparty risk. I could not obtain customer-concentration disclosure.

| FY26 | ₹ crore |
|---|---:|

| OCF as % of EBITDA | **14%** |
| Standalone trade receivables | 281.2 (from 155.9) |
| Implied DSO (standalone receivables ÷ consolidated revenue) | ~204 days |
| Working capital intensity (NWC/OI), FY25 | 50.2% |
| Cash conversion cycle | 170 days FY26 (208 days FY25; 297 days three years ago) |

Reported against this: net debt of roughly ₹110 cr, capex plans of ₹185–190 cr, and a rated facility
increase from ₹65 cr to ₹200 cr.

**The arithmetic that matters:** if incremental revenue absorbs ~45–50 paise of working capital per rupee,
then growing revenue from ₹503 cr to ₹700 cr in FY27 consumes ~₹90 cr of working capital against ~₹135 cr
of NOPAT-plus-depreciation, before ₹120 cr of planned capex. **FY27 free cash flow on my base case is
negative ₹64 cr, and FY28 is approximately zero.** The growth is real and it is being funded — by the
balance sheet, and, if it continues at this intensity, eventually by equity.

**The counter-evidence, stated as prominently:** the cash conversion cycle has fallen from 297 to 170 days
over three years. The trend is in the right direction even as the absolute receivable balance grows. If
the FY26 receivables build is a timing effect of a 36% growth year in tender-led markets rather than a
collection failure, FY27 operating cash flow will normalise sharply and the DCF's base case is too harsh.
**I cannot distinguish these two readings from the evidence available.** Resolving it requires the FY26
cash flow statement, the receivables ageing and the customer list. It is the single most valuable piece of
diligence available on this name, and it is worth more than any refinement of the discount rate.

## 7. Reverse DCF — what ₹3,774 requires

Solving for the discount rate that makes each operating case produce the traded EV of ₹4,024 cr:

| Operating case | ΔNWC 45% | ΔNWC 35% | ΔNWC 25% |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Base (FY30 revenue ₹1,393 cr) | 8.69% | 8.88% | 9.08% |
| Bull (FY30 revenue ₹1,761 cr) | 10.60% | 10.89% | 11.19% |

**At the traded price, even the bull case — revenue tripling by FY30 at 29% EBITDA margins with the
working-capital cycle materially repaired — returns 10.6–11.2%.** That is roughly 350–415bp over the
Indian 10-year G-sec, for a micro-cap formulations exporter. For comparison, Damodaran's India ERP alone
is 7.31% before any size or liquidity premium.

The price is defensible only on one of these footings, each of which must be argued rather than assumed:

1. Growth beyond my bull case — the Keytruda biosimilar and the hormone/oncology platforms scale into
   something larger than a ₹1,761 cr FY30 revenue business.
2. A structural re-rating: the market prices this as a regulated-market pharma company rather than a
   semi-regulated tender exporter, which the EU-GMP/PIC-S approvals and the Mexico/Colombia registrations
   partly support.
3. A momentum/float effect. The stock rose 122% in six months, 13% in three days, and hit an all-time high
   on 21 September 2026 on **no identified company-specific catalyst**. With 54.86% promoter-held and a
   ~₹1,760 cr free float, price is set by a thin market.

*Judgement: (3) is doing more of the work than (1) or (2). I cannot prove that, and I flag it as judgement.*

## 9. Reconciliation — why the methods disagree

The base DCF (₹955) and forward comps (₹3,180 at the median) disagree by more than 3x. Averaging them
would be worthless. The disagreement decomposes into exactly three assumptions:

| Driver | DCF base assumes | Comps implicitly assume | Value at stake |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Working capital** | 45% of incremental revenue absorbed, permanently | Not modelled at all — a P/E capitalises accrual earnings | ~₹110/share per 10pp; ~₹440 between 25% and 65% |
| **Discount rate** | 15.5% (16% cost of equity incl. a 3% size premium) | Peer multiples embed whatever rate the market applies — implicitly 9–11% (§7) | ~₹350/share between 13.5% and 15.5% |
| **Fade** | Growth fades to 6% by FY36, margin fades to 23.5% | The current multiple is held constant | The difference between base and bull DCF: ₹1,300/share |

**The reconciliation, stated as a conclusion:** the DCF and the comps are not measuring the same company.
The DCF values the company that exists — one that earned ₹17 cr of operating cash in FY26. The comps value
the company management has guided to — ₹1,500 cr of revenue at 30% margins by FY30. The recommended range
of ₹1,900–2,900 deliberately sits between them, and it is narrower than the union of the bars because:

- Below ₹1,900 requires disbelieving the FY27 guidance that Q1 FY27 has already substantially validated,
  and applying a discount rate the credit evidence (ICRA upgrade, facility increase, zero promoter pledge)
  does not support.
- Above ₹2,900 requires the FY30 guidance to be delivered **and** the working-capital cycle to be repaired
  **and** the multiple to hold — three things, none of which is yet evidenced, all of which must happen.

The midpoint of ₹2,400 implies 14.5x FY27E EBITDA and 24x FY27E earnings — a discount to the peer band
that is justified by cash conversion, float and the semi-regulated market mix, and a premium to the base
DCF that is justified by the improving conversion cycle and the pipeline optionality that the DCF's fade
assumption ignores.

## 10. What would make this valuation wrong

Stated so a reader can test it rather than take it:

1. **FY27 operating cash flow.** If OCF comes in near ₹100 cr, the receivables build was a timing effect,
   ΔNWC intensity belongs at 25–35%, and the base DCF moves to ₹1,070–1,180 with the bull case to
   ₹2,900+ — the top of my range becomes the middle. If OCF stays near ₹17 cr while revenue reaches
   ₹700 cr, the company will need external funding and the bear case is live. **This single number
   resolves more of the valuation than everything else in this memo combined.**
2. **The gross-margin-down / EBITDA-margin-up guidance.** Gross margin is guided down 500–900bp to 47–51%
   while EBITDA margin is guided up to 27–28%. If operating leverage does not deliver that, FY27 EBITDA
   is nearer ₹160 cr than ₹192 cr and every forward multiple in §8 is applied to the wrong number.
3. **Customer and geographic concentration.** Not disclosed in anything I could reach. Semi-regulated
   tender business is typically concentrated. A single large defaulting counterparty in the ₹281 cr
   receivable book would be a material impairment of the equity, not a working-capital timing issue.
4. **The share count.** Inferred, not verified (§0). A past corporate action invalidates every per-share
   figure here — though not the enterprise-value conclusions.
5. **The size premium.** Removing the 3% judgement premium lifts the base DCF from ₹955 to ~₹1,300. It is
   the most contestable single input I have chosen, and I have chosen it deliberately high.
6. **Pipeline optionality is valued at zero in the DCF.** The Keytruda biosimilar (targeted pre-end-2028)
   and the hormone facility (₹150 cr revenue potential) enter the forecast only through the revenue growth
   rates. Per technique 9 of `valuation-techniques`, a deferred-decision asset of this kind should be
   valued separately with stated volatility. I did not, because I have no evidence on trial cost, timing
   or probability of approval. **This biases my range low, and a buyer should say so.**

## 11. If this becomes a transaction — India regime

Flagged per `india-transaction-regime`; **every threshold below requires confirmation against the current
instrument, particularly given the December 2025 SAST amendments**, which I could not read in full.

- **Open offer (SEBI SAST 2011).** Promoters hold 54.86%. Any acquisition of 25%+ of voting rights, or of
  control, triggers a mandatory open offer for a minimum **26% of total voting share capital**, with
  escrow up front. At the traded price, a 54.86% promoter stake costs ~₹2,150 cr and the mandatory open
  offer adds ~₹1,020 cr — **an affordability envelope of ~₹3,200 cr before any premium**, against my
  recommended equity range of ₹1,970–3,010 cr. Any buyer sizing against the promoter stake alone will
  mis-size the transaction by roughly half.
- **Open-offer price as a floor.** The Regulation 8 formula, not negotiation, sets what minorities receive.
  With the share at an all-time high after a 122% six-month run, the historical-price legs of that formula
  are at their most expensive in the company's history. *Timing observation: this is the worst moment in
  three years to launch a control transaction on this stock.*
- **FEMA pricing.** A non-resident acquirer may not pay **below** fair value; a non-resident seller may not
  receive **above** it. Deferred consideration is capped at **25% of total consideration for 18 months** —
  any earn-out bridging the §9 valuation gap must fit inside that envelope.
- **Press Note 3.** Any acquirer with beneficial ownership in a land-border country needs prior government
  approval regardless of sector — the test reaches through Singapore and Mauritius vehicles.
- **CCI.** At an equity value above ₹2,000 cr the **deal value threshold** is engaged; with a target that
  plainly has substantial Indian operations, notification should be assumed. Green Channel may be
  available absent overlaps.
- **Section 79 (loss carryforward).** Not applicable — this is a listed company in which the public are
  substantially interested. No NOL value at risk from a change of control.
- **Tax regime.** The s.115BAA election is **inferred** from the ~25% effective rate, not verified. Confirm
  before any cash-tax build.
- **Control premium.** I found **no sourced dataset of Indian open-offer control premia** in this session
  and have therefore **not applied one**. The range in §1 is a minority-basis, going-concern range. A
  control valuation requires the premium to be built from the specific synergies and control benefits
  available to a named buyer (technique 4), not from a sector average — and stacking a generic premium on
  these numbers would be exactly the error that technique warns against.
