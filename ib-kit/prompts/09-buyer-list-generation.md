# Buyer List & Buyer Outreach Prompts

**Layer:** Insight · **Engagement contexts:** M&A Advisory, Strategic Alternatives, Restructuring
**Pairs with:** `skills/buyer-universe-mapping`, `agents/buyer-universe-agent`, `workflows/buyer-list.md`

## A. Universe construction

1. **Build the buyer universe** — "Build the full buyer universe for a sale of `<Company>`. Segment into strategic — direct competitors, adjacent players, vertical integrators, new entrants — and financial — sector-focused sponsors, generalists, growth equity, family offices, pension and sovereign funds, existing portfolio companies. For each: name, type, rationale, estimated capacity, likely interest level."
2. **Direct strategics** — "List every direct competitor of `<Company>` globally with revenue above `<$X>`m. For each, state the strategic logic of acquiring `<Company>` in one sentence and the reason they might decline in another."
3. **Adjacent strategics** — "Identify acquirers from adjacent categories for whom `<Company>` is a capability, channel or geographic entry. Explain the entry logic for each."
4. **Vertical integrators** — "Identify `<Company>`'s customers and suppliers who have a credible reason to integrate. Assess whether their historical behaviour supports it."
5. **New-entrant buyers** — "Identify buyers from outside `<sector>` — adjacent industries, technology platforms, international players seeking entry — with the precedent that shows they do this."
6. **International buyers** — "Identify credible buyers from `<regions>`. Assess FDI screening, capital-control and political risk for each, and rank by execution certainty rather than headline appetite."
7. **Sector-focused sponsors** — "List private equity firms with a stated `<sector>` thesis. For each: fund size, current fund vintage, dry powder, typical cheque size, relevant portfolio companies and the partner who owns the sector."
8. **Portfolio-company buyers** — "Identify sponsor-owned platforms in `<sector>` for which `<Company>` is a logical add-on. Name the platform, the sponsor, the entry date and the consolidation thesis."
9. **Long-hold capital** — "Identify family offices, sovereign funds, pension funds and long-hold vehicles active in `<sector>`. Assess their appetite for a control position and their process behaviour."
10. **Management buyout** — "Assess whether an MBO is credible: management's equity capacity, likely sponsor backing, and the conflict-management protocol required."

## B. Assessment and prioritisation

11. **Capacity test** — "For each buyer, assess financial capacity: balance-sheet cash, leverage headroom, market cap relative to deal size, dry powder, and whether the deal needs shareholder approval. Remove anyone who cannot actually pay."
12. **Willingness-to-pay ranking** — "Rank buyers by estimated willingness to pay, built from their specific synergy set — not a generic premium. Show the synergy estimate that drives each ranking."
13. **Strategic urgency** — "For each strategic buyer, assess urgency: a capability gap they have publicly acknowledged, competitive pressure, a stated M&A ambition, or an activist. Urgency moves price more than fit does."
14. **Acquisition track record** — "For each buyer, list deals done in the last 5 years: size, multiple, process behaviour, speed, and whether they closed what they signed."
15. **Process behaviour profile** — "Profile each Tier 1 buyer's behaviour in processes: do they bid early or late, bump, lead with structure, demand exclusivity, re-trade after diligence? Cite the deal that shows it."
16. **Antitrust screen** — "For each strategic buyer, assess the combined share in each overlapping product and geographic market, the likely review path, the remedy exposure and the timeline. Rank by regulatory risk."
17. **Regulatory and FDI screen** — "Screen each buyer for FDI, national-security and sector-regulator approval requirements. Identify any buyer whose clearance risk makes them a stalking horse rather than a real bidder."
18. **Cultural and continuity fit** — "Assess each buyer against the seller's non-price objectives: employee continuity, brand retention, site commitments, founder legacy, customer service continuity."
19. **Confidentiality risk** — "Assess the confidentiality risk of contacting each buyer: competitive sensitivity of the information they would see, leak history, and employee or customer overlap. Recommend which get in-scope diligence and when."
20. **Tier the list** — "Tier the universe: Tier 1 (contact first, highest probability and price), Tier 2 (contact in wave 2), Tier 3 (only if the process underperforms), Do Not Contact (with the reason). Give the count and rationale per tier."

## C. Process design

21. **Process recommendation** — "Given this buyer universe, recommend broad auction, targeted process or bilateral negotiation. Justify by reference to the number of credible buyers, confidentiality sensitivity, and the seller's speed-versus-price preference."
22. **Wave design** — "Design the outreach waves: who is contacted in each wave, in what sequence, with what timing gap, and what triggers moving to the next wave."
23. **Timetable** — "Build the process timetable from launch to signing: teaser, NDA, CIM, management presentations, first-round bids, data room opening, second-round diligence, final bids, exclusivity, SPA negotiation, signing. Give dates and owners."
24. **Tension design** — "Identify the minimum number of credible second-round bidders needed to sustain price tension, and the specific action to take if fewer materialise."
25. **Stalking horse** — "Assess whether to secure a stalking-horse bid before launch. State the price-certainty benefit versus the tension cost."
26. **Pre-emptive bid protocol** — "Define the protocol for handling a pre-emptive offer: the threshold at which we recommend accepting, the market-check requirement, and the board's fiduciary documentation."

## D. Outreach materials and execution

27. **Teaser** — "Write a one-page anonymous teaser for `<Company>`: investment highlights, financial summary, transaction overview. It must be compelling without being identifiable — test it by asking whether a sector insider could name the company."
28. **Buyer-specific positioning** — "For `<Buyer>`, write the 200-word positioning of `<Company>` that speaks to their specific strategic gap. Then do it for the next four buyers, each genuinely different."
29. **Outreach call script** — "Write the first-call script for `<Buyer>`: the opening, the three facts that earn the next meeting, the process framing, and the response to 'send me the materials and I'll look'."
30. **Outreach email** — "Draft the outreach email to `<Buyer>`'s head of corporate development: under 150 words, specific to them, with a clear ask."
31. **NDA position** — "Draft our standard NDA position for this process: term, non-solicit, standstill, permitted disclosures, residual-knowledge clause. Note which terms we concede and which we do not."
32. **CIM outline** — "Outline the confidential information memorandum for `<Company>`: section structure, page allocation, the three messages every section reinforces, and the facts we lead with."
33. **Management presentation** — "Design the management presentation: narrative arc, who presents what, the 5 questions management must answer perfectly, and the rehearsal plan."
34. **Bid process letter** — "Draft the first-round bid process letter: what we require in the bid, the format, the deadline, the evaluation criteria and the reservation of rights."

## E. Managing the process

35. **Tracker design** — "Design the buyer tracker: company, contact, tier, status, NDA date, CIM sent, questions outstanding, site visit, bid received, bid value, structure, conditions, next action, next action date."
36. **Bid comparison grid** — "Build the bid comparison grid across all first-round bids: headline value, net value to shareholders after adjustments, structure, conditionality, financing certainty, regulatory risk, timing, and employee commitments. Rank on certainty-adjusted value, not headline."
37. **Bid evaluation memo** — "Write the board memo evaluating first-round bids: who bid what, what each bid really means after adjusting for structure and conditions, who we advance and why, and who we push for more."
38. **Negotiation strategy per bidder** — "For each second-round bidder, define the negotiation strategy: their likely maximum, the lever that moves them, the concession we can afford, and the point at which we stop."
39. **Dropout diagnosis** — "`<Buyer>` has withdrawn. Diagnose the real reason from their behaviour and questions, assess whether it signals a diligence problem that will affect other bidders, and recommend the response."
40. **Re-trade defence** — "`<Buyer>` is attempting to re-trade on `<issue>`. Assess the legitimacy of the issue, quantify the defensible adjustment, and prepare the response including the walk-away position."
41. **Process status report** — "Write the weekly seller update: stage, buyers active, bids received, issues arising, decisions needed from the board this week, and the outlook on timing and value."
42. **Post-process review** — "After signing, review the process: who was contacted, who engaged, who bid, price progression, what created tension, and what we would do differently. Write it while it is fresh."
