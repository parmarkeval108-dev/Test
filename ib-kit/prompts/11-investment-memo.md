# Investment Memo & Committee Materials Prompts

**Layer:** Insight · **Engagement contexts:** All
**Pairs with:** `skills/investment-memo-writing`, `agents/investment-memo-agent`, `workflows/investment-committee.md`

## A. Structure and thesis

1. **Memo outline** — "Outline the investment committee memo for `<Transaction>`: sections, the argument each section makes, page allocation, and the decision the memo is asking the committee to take. Write the outline before a single paragraph of prose."
2. **The recommendation up front** — "Write the recommendation in the first 100 words: what we recommend, at what price, on what structure, and the three reasons. A reader who stops after the first page must know the answer."
3. **Investment thesis** — "State the investment thesis in three sentences. The first says what this business is; the second says why it wins; the third says why now."
4. **Thesis pillars** — "Break the thesis into 3–5 pillars. For each: the claim, the evidence, the metric that proves it, and the fact that would disprove it."
5. **Executive summary** — "Write a one-page executive summary: transaction, thesis, financials, valuation, returns, risks, recommendation, and the approvals sought. Stand-alone and self-sufficient."
6. **Decision framing** — "State precisely what the committee is approving: the price, the structure, the funding, the authority delegated, and the conditions attached. Ambiguity here causes the worst outcomes."

## B. Business and market sections

7. **Business description** — "Write the business description: what the company sells, to whom, how it makes money, and where it sits in the value chain. A reader with no sector knowledge must understand it."
8. **How it makes money** — "Write the unit economics section: the profit on one unit of whatever this business sells, the drivers of that profit, and how it scales."
9. **Market section** — "Write the market section: size, growth, structure, drivers and the company's position. Every claim carries a source."
10. **Competitive position** — "Write the competitive position section: who they compete with, why they win, what the evidence is, and how durable it is."
11. **Management assessment** — "Write the management section: track record, capability against the plan's requirements, incentives post-transaction, gaps, and the honest assessment of whether this team can execute this plan."
12. **Why this company** — "Answer directly: of all the assets in this sector, why this one? What makes it scarce or superior?"
13. **Why now** — "Answer directly: why is this the right moment? Identify what changes if we wait 12 months."

## C. Financial sections

14. **Historical financials** — "Present the historical financials with the story behind them: what drove each inflection, what is cyclical, what is structural, and what management would prefer we not notice."
15. **Quality of earnings summary** — "Summarise the QoE findings: reported EBITDA, adjustments accepted, adjustments rejected, the EBITDA we underwrite, and the gap to the seller's number."
16. **Forecast presentation** — "Present the forecast with the drivers visible: for each year, the revenue build, margin path and cash conversion, and where our case differs from management's."
17. **Our case versus management's** — "Table management's plan against our underwriting case line by line, with the reason for each haircut and the total value effect."
18. **Valuation section** — "Present the valuation: comps, precedents, DCF and LBO, the football field, the range, and the price we recommend with the reason it is inside that range."
19. **Returns section** — "Present returns: base, upside and downside IRR and MOIC, the attribution, and the three assumptions on which the return depends."
20. **Financing section** — "Present the financing: structure, pricing, terms, credit stats at entry and exit, covenant headroom, and the market evidence that the structure clears."
21. **Synergy section** — "Present synergies: the total, the evidence grading, the phasing, the cost to achieve, the net NPV, and what we are not counting."
22. **Sensitivity presentation** — "Present the sensitivities that matter: the two variables that decide the outcome, in a grid, with the base case marked and the breakeven identified."

## D. Risk and mitigation

23. **Risk section** — "Write the risk section: the 8 risks that matter, each with likelihood, impact in dollars, mitigation, the residual exposure after mitigation, and who owns it. Generic risks dilute the real ones — cut them."
24. **The three things that kill this deal** — "Name the three things that would actually destroy value here, the probability of each, and what we do about them. Be specific enough to be uncomfortable."
25. **Downside case narrative** — "Describe the downside case as a story: what happens, in what order, over what period, and what our equity is worth at the end of it."
26. **Pre-mortem** — "It is three years from now and this investment has failed. Write the account of why, using only risks visible today."
27. **Key-person risk** — "Identify the individuals whose departure would materially damage the investment case, quantify the damage, and set out the retention structure."
28. **Downside protection** — "Identify the structural protections available: price adjustment, earn-out, escrow, indemnity, preferred structure, board control. Recommend the package."
29. **Exit assessment** — "Assess exit: the likely buyer universe in 5 years, the multiple they would pay, the evidence that this exit is available, and what would close the window."

## E. Writing quality and review

30. **Numbers discipline pass** — "Review the memo: every number must have a source and a period, every percentage a base, and every forecast a driver. List every figure that fails this test."
31. **Assertion audit** — "Identify every assertion in this memo that is stated without evidence. For each, either supply the evidence or mark it as judgement."
32. **Cut the hedging** — "Rewrite this memo removing every hedge that avoids taking a position — 'may', 'could potentially', 'is expected to' where no one expects it. Say what we think and own it."
33. **Length discipline** — "This memo is `<X>` pages. Cut it to `<Y>` without losing any decision-relevant content. Report what you removed."
34. **Plain language pass** — "Rewrite the memo so that a director who is not a banker can follow every argument. Keep the technical precision; remove the jargon that carries no information."
35. **Consistency check** — "Check every number that appears more than once in this memo and in the appendices. Report every inconsistency with page references."
36. **Internal contradiction sweep** — "Find places where the memo argues one thing in the thesis and another in the risks — for example, a pricing-power claim next to a competitive-intensity risk. Resolve each."
37. **The sceptic's read** — "Read this memo as the most sceptical committee member. Write the ten objections you would raise and mark which the memo currently fails to answer."
38. **Devil's advocate memo** — "Write the 500-word memo arguing against this transaction, using only facts already in the memo. If it is persuasive, the memo is incomplete."
39. **Committee Q&A prep** — "Write the 20 questions the committee will ask, the answer to each, and the supporting page or exhibit."
40. **Conditions and next steps** — "Draft the conditions of approval, the outstanding diligence, the approvals still required, and the timetable to signing, each with an owner and a date."
41. **Appendix design** — "Specify the appendices: what goes in each, why it is not in the body, and how the body cross-references it."
42. **Final read-through** — "Read the memo end to end as the decision-maker. State the decision you would take on this document alone, and identify the single addition that would most improve your confidence."
