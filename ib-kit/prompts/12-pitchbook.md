# Pitchbook & Client Materials Prompts

**Layer:** Insight · **Engagement contexts:** All
**Pairs with:** `skills/pitchbook-construction`, `agents/pitchbook-drafting-agent`, `workflows/pitchbook.md`

## A. Strategy before slides

1. **Define the objective** — "Before any page is built: what is this pitch asking the client to do, who in the room decides, what do they already believe, and what must change in their mind? Answer these four, then outline."
2. **Storyline first** — "Write the pitch as a 10-sentence argument before it is a deck. Each sentence becomes a section. If the argument does not hold in 10 sentences, no amount of design saves it."
3. **Audience map** — "Map the room: each attendee, their role, what they care about, their likely objection, and the page that addresses it."
4. **Kill the generic** — "Review this outline and delete every page that would appear in a pitch to any other client in this sector. What remains is the actual pitch."
5. **Page inventory** — "Build the page inventory: page number, title, the single message, the exhibit, the data source and the owner. Approve this before anyone opens a template."
6. **Message hierarchy** — "For each section, state the one message. If a section has three messages, it is three sections or two of them are cut."

## B. Standard pages

7. **Cover and agenda** — "Draft the cover and agenda. The agenda should preview the argument, not list generic section names."
8. **Executive summary page** — "Build the executive summary page: the recommendation, three supporting points, and the ask. A reader who sees only this page must get the argument."
9. **Situation assessment** — "Build the situation page: where the client is now, the three facts that define the situation, and the implication. Show them their own business in a way they have not seen it."
10. **Market overview** — "Build the market page: one sizing chart, one growth or share chart, three takeaways. Never more than three."
11. **Competitive positioning** — "Build the positioning page: a two-axis map or a benchmarking table, with the client's position marked and the movement over three years shown."
12. **Company benchmarking** — "Build the benchmarking page: the client against peers on growth, margin, returns, leverage and valuation, with the client highlighted and the outlier metrics annotated."
13. **Trading performance** — "Build the share-price page: three-year price and volume, indexed against peers and the sector index, with the five events that moved it annotated."
14. **Shareholder analysis** — "Build the ownership page: top holders, changes in the last four quarters, holder style classification, and what it implies for a transaction vote."
15. **Analyst perspective** — "Build the sell-side page: ratings distribution, target-price range, consensus versus our estimates, and the two bear-case arguments in the market."
16. **Valuation football field** — "Build the football field: each methodology as a bar, with the low and high driver labelled, the current price marked, and the implied range stated in the header."
17. **Strategic alternatives** — "Build the alternatives page: status quo, organic investment, acquisition, divestment, merger, sale, recapitalisation — each with the value implication and the principal risk."
18. **Transaction structure** — "Build the structure page: the proposed structure as a simple diagram, with consideration, financing, ownership and governance outcomes stated."
19. **Target or buyer profiles** — "Build the profile pages: one page per name with description, financials, ownership, rationale and a one-line view. Consistent layout across all of them."
20. **Precedent transactions page** — "Build the precedents page: the table, the median, and a chart of multiples over time with the read for this situation."
21. **Financial projections page** — "Build the projections page: the forecast with drivers shown, our case against management's, and the assumption footnote that makes the numbers defensible."
22. **Financing markets page** — "Build the financing page: current market conditions, available structures, indicative terms, and recent comparable financings."
23. **Process and timetable** — "Build the timetable page: phases, key milestones, decision points, and the client's obligations at each stage."
24. **Credentials page** — "Build the credentials page: only transactions genuinely relevant to this mandate, with the reason each is relevant stated in four words."
25. **Team page** — "Build the team page: who works on this, what each person does, and the specific relevant experience of each. No filler bios."

## C. Exhibit craft

26. **Choose the chart** — "For this data `<data>`, recommend the chart type and explain why the alternatives are worse. Composition, comparison, distribution and correlation each have a right answer."
27. **Chart discipline** — "Review this exhibit: is the axis honest, is the scale consistent, is the data labelled, is the source cited with a date, is the takeaway in the title? Fix every failure."
28. **Action titles** — "Rewrite every page title as a conclusion, not a label. 'Revenue growth' becomes 'Revenue growth has outpaced the sector for six consecutive quarters'. Cap at 12 words."
29. **Data-ink reduction** — "Strip this exhibit of everything that does not carry information: gridlines, 3D effects, redundant legends, decorative colour. Show the before and after."
30. **Annotation** — "Annotate this chart with the three things the audience would otherwise miss. Annotations do the work that a presenter's voice cannot do in a leave-behind."
31. **Consistency pass** — "Check the deck for consistency: fonts, sizes, colours, number formatting, decimal places, units, date formats, legend positions, source-line format. Produce the defect list with page numbers."
32. **Footnote discipline** — "Write the footnote for every exhibit: source, date, methodology, and any adjustment made. A page without a source line is not ready."

## D. Review and delivery

33. **Page-level review** — "For each page, state the message, then check whether the exhibit proves it. List every page where the exhibit does not support the title."
34. **Flow test** — "Read only the page titles in sequence. Do they make a complete argument on their own? If not, the deck does not flow — fix the titles or the order."
35. **Cut test** — "Identify the 20% of pages that could be removed without weakening the argument. Move them to the appendix."
36. **Fact-check pass** — "Verify every figure on every page against its source. Produce a table of page, figure, source and verification status. Anything unverified comes off the page."
37. **The client's objections** — "Write the five objections the client will raise, the page each relates to, and the response. If a response is not supported by a page, build it into the appendix."
38. **Competitive pitch read** — "Assume two other banks are pitching the same mandate with the same public data. Identify what in this deck they cannot replicate. If the answer is nothing, the pitch is not finished."
39. **Senior banker review** — "Review this deck as the senior banker who will present it: what will you be asked that you cannot answer, what page will you skip, and what page is missing?"
40. **Delivery brief** — "Write the presenter's brief: the 60-second opening, the three pages that must land, the pages to skip if time is short, and the close with the specific ask."
41. **Leave-behind version** — "Adapt the deck for an audience reading without a presenter: expand the titles, add the connective text, and move the detail forward."
42. **Post-meeting follow-up** — "From these meeting notes `<notes>`, draft the follow-up: what we committed to, the answers to their open questions, the next step with a date, and the materials to send."
