## 2. What the business actually is

Design and manufacture of RF and microwave sub-systems — not complete systems. The
company sells into four end-markets: **defence** (radar sub-systems, electronic warfare,
missile sub-systems, telemetry), **space** (satellite sub-systems, work begun 2003),
**meteorology and hydrology** (weather radar for IMD), and **exports** (MMICs, MEMS,
sub-assemblies).

The economic position is that of a tier-2 supplier to India's defence primes and labs.
Revenue is earned on milestone-based contracts with government customers, which is why
the working-capital cycle reads the way it does (section 7). The company does not own
the platform, does not hold the prime contract, and in most programmes is one of several
qualified sources — so its pricing power comes from qualification and indigenous-content
requirements rather than from a monopoly position.

### The single most important fact in this table

Over FY23→FY26, revenue compounded at **12.5%** but PAT compounded at **40.2%**
*(both derived)*. The gap is margin: EBITDA margin rose **10.6 percentage points** in
three years. Had FY26 revenue been earned at the FY23 margin, EBITDA would have been
₹210 crore against ₹334 crore actual — **₹124 crore, 37% of FY26 EBITDA, is margin
rather than volume** *(derived)*.

A 10.6-point margin expansion does not repeat. Any forecast that carries the FY23–FY26
earnings CAGR forward is, without saying so, assuming a second margin expansion of
similar size on top of a base already at 28.7%. That assumption should be stated
explicitly and defended, or the forecast should revert toward revenue growth.

reasons given — customer inspections, specification sign-off, material availability —
are the same reasons that will govern the HAL ramp. That is the specific thing to test

**State the basis every time this number is used.**

### The HAL Uttam award **[UNTIED]**

On **31 July 2026**, HAL placed an order for **122 Active Antenna Array Units and 121
interface frames** for the indigenous Uttam AESA fire-control radar, worth
**₹2,205.23 crore**, for the second batch of **97 Tejas Mk1A** aircraft. Delivery is
planned to complete by **2031**.

Straight-lined to 2031 that is ~₹441 crore a year — **38% of FY26 consolidated revenue**
*(derived)*. Straight-lining is almost certainly wrong: a programme of this type is
front-loaded with development, qualification and first-article acceptance and back-loaded
with rate production, so the early years will carry cost without proportionate revenue.
**The phasing is the number that matters and it is not public.** Until the delivery
schedule is seen, no year-by-year revenue build off this order is defensible.

Two further points that cut against the headline. The first 83 Tejas Mk1A retain the
Israeli EL/M-2052 radar; Uttam was moved to the follow-on 97. The award is therefore
contingent on a fleet order that is itself downstream of HAL's own delivery record on
Mk1A, which has slipped repeatedly. And the order is a single customer, single
programme, five-year concentration on top of an already concentrated book.

## 5. The demerger

Board approved a scheme of arrangement on **10 June 2026**:

- **Demerged undertaking:** Space, Meteorology and Hydrology business, on a going-concern basis
- **Resulting company:** Astra Space Technologies Private Limited (ASTPL), currently wholly owned
- **Entitlement:** **1:1**, mirror shareholding, **no cash consideration**
- **Size of demerged business:** FY26 turnover ~**₹157 crore** = **13.58%** of turnover; order backlog ~**₹716 crore**
- **Approvals:** NCLT, SEBI, BSE, NSE and others
- **Intent:** separate listing of ASTPL

**The cross-check that works, and what it means.** The demerged business is 13.58% of
revenue but ₹716 crore of backlog — **33% of the ₹2,156 crore standalone June order
book** *(derived)*, which ties closely to the reported 34% space/metrology/hydrology
share of that book. The two disclosures corroborate each other.

The consequence is that **the residual listed entity loses a quarter more of its order
book than of its revenue**, and emerges more concentrated on defence and on the
DRDO/HAL/BEL relationship than the current accounts show. Anyone valuing ASTRAMICRO as
a going concern post-scheme is valuing a narrower, more concentrated business than the
FY26 consolidated statements describe. That is not a criticism of the scheme — a
separately listed space business may well be worth more apart — but it does mean the
current market capitalisation is not a valuation of the post-scheme listed line.

On timing: under the Companies Act 2013 a scheme requires NCLT sanction, and those
timelines run to many months and sit outside the parties' control. Treat NCLT as the
long pole; do not benchmark against a Western statutory-merger timetable.

## 6. Ownership, control and what that opens up

**Shareholding as at December 2025 [UNTIED]**

| Holder | % |
|---|---:|
| Promoters | **6.54%** |
| FIIs | 10.74% |
| DIIs | 16.09% |
| Public / retail | 66.63% |
| *of which mutual funds* | *15.37%, rising* |

A ₹15,700 crore listed defence business with a **6.54% promoter holding** has, in
substance, **no controlling shareholder**. Control sits with the board, not with a
family block. Three consequences:

1. **The company is structurally open to an approach.** There is no promoter block that
   can refuse. Deliverability of a control transaction turns on the board and on
   institutional holders, not on a single negotiation.
2. **But the cost of control is set by statute, not by the stake.** Under SEBI (SAST)
   2011, crossing 25% of voting rights, or acquiring control, triggers a mandatory open
   offer for a minimum **26% of total voting share capital**, with escrow posted up
   front. At today's market capitalisation, 25% is ~₹3,935 crore and the open offer a
   further ~₹4,093 crore — **~₹8,000 crore before any premium, and before any control
   premium on the open-offer price formula** *(derived)*. An acquirer who can fund 25%
   cannot necessarily fund the trigger.
3. **Promoter-group composition and any pledge are unverified.** The 6.54% is a single
   aggregate from a secondary source. The promoter-group table, any inter-se agreement
   and the pledge position all need to be read off the exchange filing before
   deliverability is scored — pledging is a live distress signal and is separately
   disclosed.

**Not assessed:** foreign ownership limits applicable to an Indian defence manufacturer.
I did not verify the current sectoral FDI cap or approval route and will not state one
from memory. This is decisive for any cross-border buyer and must be established before
a buyer universe is built. Rafael is Israeli and so is not caught by Press Note 3; a
buyer with beneficial ownership in a land-border country would be, through any
intermediate holding structure.

## 7. Working capital — the structural feature

**[UNTIED]**

| | FY25 | FY26 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Debtor days | 273 | **216** |
| Inventory days | 213 | **193** |
| Creditor days | 27 | 36 |
| **Cash conversion cycle** | **459** | **374** |

At 31 March 2026, standalone: trade receivables **₹687 crore**, inventories
**₹609 crore**, current borrowings **₹224 crore**, non-current borrowings **₹63 crore**.

Receivables alone are **59% of FY26 consolidated revenue** *(derived)*. The cycle
improved by 85 days and is still **374 days** — the business finances more than a year of
trading. This is not a working-capital problem to be fixed; it is the operating model of
a milestone-billed government supplier, and it is the reason FY26 operating cash flow of
₹370 crore was reported as a notable event rather than a routine one.

The forward risk is specific: **the HAL ramp will consume working capital before it
generates cash.** A programme delivering to 2031 on milestone acceptance, in a business
already carrying 216 debtor days and 193 inventory days, will require funding through the
build. Current borrowings are ₹224 crore. Whether that headroom is sufficient cannot be
judged without the consolidated balance sheet, the cash position and the sanctioned
facility limits — none of which I could retrieve.

  — a payout of roughly **12% of consolidated PAT** *(derived from an implied ~9.74 crore

**The concurrency is the point.** Within roughly twelve months the company is changing
managing director, separating a third of its order book into a new listed entity, and
beginning execution on the largest order in its history. Each is manageable alone. The
same finance and programme-management bench is being asked to carry all three, and the
Q1 FY27 miss was attributed to exactly the operational disciplines — inspection
readiness, specification closure, material availability — that this period will stress.
The outgoing MD moving to the demerged entity also means the residual listed company
loses its most experienced executive to the part being separated.

## 10. Where the numbers do not reconcile

This is stated rather than smoothed over, because the smoothing is where profiles go
wrong.

FY26 consolidated is reported as revenue ₹1,162.80 cr, EBITDA ₹334 cr (28.7%), PAT
₹192.97 cr — and, from a different source, an **EBIT margin of 27.20%** (FY25: 24.82%).

Working it through:

1. EBIT margin 27.20% on ₹1,162.80 cr → EBIT ≈ **₹316.3 cr**.
2. EBITDA ₹334 cr − EBIT ₹316.3 cr → implied **D&A ≈ ₹17.7 cr**, i.e. 1.5% of revenue.
   That is low for a business with this manufacturing and test asset base.
3. At the s.115BAA effective rate of **25.17%**, a PAT of ₹192.97 cr implies **PBT ≈
   ₹257.9 cr**.
4. EBIT ₹316.3 cr − PBT ₹257.9 cr → **₹58.4 cr** of net interest and other below-the-line
   items.
5. Against reported total borrowings of ₹287 cr (standalone), ₹58.4 cr implies an
   effective borrowing cost near **20%**. Not credible.

So at least one of these is on a different basis from the others: the EBIT margin may be
standalone against consolidated EBITDA; the borrowings are standalone while the earnings
are consolidated; the tax regime election is unverified (the alternative rate is ~34.94%,
which would change step 3 materially); or share of JV results is being included
differently across the two sources.

**I have not resolved it and I have not adjusted any figure to make it close.** It
resolves against the audited FY26 consolidated statements and notes, and until it does,
no EBIT-based or EV/EBIT multiple for this company should be relied on.

## 11. What weakens the case

Set out with the same prominence as the order book.

- **Delivery fell while guidance rose.** Q1 FY27 revenue −10.7%, PAT −24%, and FY27
  guidance raised from 10–15% to 15–20% in the same release.
- **The earnings record is a margin story, not a volume story.** 37% of FY26 EBITDA comes
  from margin expansion since FY23. That engine cannot run again from 28.7%.
- **374-day cash conversion cycle**, receivables at 59% of revenue — and the largest order
  in company history now to be funded through it.
- **68.9% of standalone revenue is government**; DRDO and the DPSU block alone are 50.9%.
  Private customers are 3.0%.
- **The HAL order is contingent on the 97-aircraft follow-on batch**, on a programme
  (Tejas Mk1A) with a record of schedule slippage, and its year-by-year phasing is not
  public.
- **Three disruptions at once** — MD succession, demerger, largest-ever programme ramp —
  with the outgoing MD moving to the demerged entity.
- **The JV was downgraded.** ICRA cut Astra Rafael Comsys on a change of approach from
  parent-support to standalone assessment, citing moderate scale, working-capital
  intensity funded by extended vendor/promoter credit and customer advances, a leveraged
  capital structure, and customer concentration on MoD arms and Rafael. *The rating level
  and action date are unverified — I could not open the rationale.* ARC is not small
  relative to the parent: FY26 sales ₹360 cr, and related-party transactions with AMPL of
  ₹160.09 cr in FY26.
- **ARC is guided flat-to-down.** FY26 sales ₹360 cr against an FY27 estimate of "₹350
  crore plus" at ~12% PBT.
- **The price is above the most recent, most positive sell-side target** I could find.
- **Promoter holding of 6.54%** gives thin insider alignment. It is simultaneously the
  strongest argument for optionality and the weakest for alignment; both should be said.

## 12. Assumptions that change the conclusion if wrong

1. **HAL order phasing is linear to 2031.** Almost certainly wrong. If the programme is
   back-loaded, FY27–FY29 revenue carries far less of the ₹2,205 crore than the headline
   suggests, while working capital is consumed early.
2. **FY23–FY26 figures are consolidated throughout.** FY25 revenue ties to the reported
   consolidated comparative, which is why I treat the series as consolidated — but FY23
   and FY24 are not independently confirmed on that basis.
3. **The 28.7% FY26 EBITDA margin is sustainable.** If mix reverts — more HAL hardware,
   less high-margin export and MMIC content — the margin falls and the multiple math in
   section 9 gets worse quickly.
4. **s.115BAA has been elected** (25.17% effective). If not, the effective rate is nearer
   34.94% and every cash-tax build changes by ~1,000 basis points.
5. **The demerger completes broadly as approved.** NCLT timing and any modification to
   the asset/liability split change both entities' balance sheets.
6. **The 6.54% promoter figure is current and complete.** It is a December 2025 aggregate
   from a secondary source; a later filing may differ, and the pledge position is unknown.

## Basis discipline

Indian listed companies report standalone and consolidated, and the two circulate
interchangeably in market commentary. In this profile:

- Sections 3 (annual table), 5 and 9 are **consolidated** unless marked otherwise.
- Q4 FY26, Q1 FY27, the customer mix, the order-book splits, working capital and the
  balance-sheet items are **standalone**, as reported.
- The EV/EBITDA and P/E figures in section 9 therefore mix a consolidated earnings
  denominator with standalone borrowings. That is flagged there and is one of the
  reconciliation failures in section 10. It must be fixed before any comparable-company
  work uses these numbers.
